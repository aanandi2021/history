Amit's Notes of the History of the World

Visualization mockups · WW2 proof of concept · 2026-10-04

→ The viewer (A + B combined)

These mockups led to the real viewer in app/: swimlanes and map together, chapters that zoom both, cards with links to the vault, Wikipedia, Calibre and the library, and two periods (WW2 and Russia 1480–1941).

Three ways of looking at the same hand-entered WW2 dataset (66 events, 14 leaders, 12 books). All three keep a Canada lane. They are meant to be compared rather than chosen between — the final viewer will probably combine two of them.

A · Swimlanes

Ideas 1, 2, 5 together. Leaders' reigns over their lifetimes, five theatre lanes, the books you'd read as bars across the years they cover, and your family's lifetimes. A "meanwhile" cursor follows the mouse and lists what was happening everywhere on that date, who was in power, and how old each family member was.

Best for: understanding simultaneity. Weakest on a phone.

B · Map + timeline

Idea 3. A world map coloured by Axis, occupied, Allied and Soviet control, driven by a slider or Play, with a timeline strip underneath that moves in step.

Best for: geography — seeing why Leningrad, Stalingrad and Singapore mattered. The map is simplified to modern whole-country borders for now.

C · Scroll story

The same events read top to bottom, one row per month, with the theatres as columns and Canada first. Changes of leader appear inline and each year opens with who was in power and which books start there.

Best for: reading through a period on the iPad. Closest to the Obsidian notes themselves.

Idea 6 · Notes drive the viewer

The Obsidian notes stay the source of truth. Each entry carries region, start, end; a build script (scripts/build_data.py) reads them and writes the JSON these pages load. The next step is to give events and books their own frontmatter so the vault fills the lanes rather than a hand-written file.
